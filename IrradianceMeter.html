ANDROID APPLICATION · PROJECT NOTES

Sun Irradiance Meter

A practical solar irradiance estimator built around the light and motion sensors already in your phone.

Android Works offline Google Play
View on Google Play ↗ Explore how it works ↓

01 / MEASUREMENT

From ambient light to an estimate

The app reads the phone's ambient light sensor and converts its illuminance reading, measured in lux, into an estimated solar irradiance value in watts per square metre (W/m²). Motion-sensor data supports tilt correction, helping account for the angle of the phone while taking a reading.

The result is an estimate based on the phone's sensors and the app's calibration settings. It is designed for convenient, comparative outdoor readings, not as a replacement for dedicated calibrated solar-measurement equipment.

02 / LIVE ESTIMATES

A steadier view of changing light

Sunlight and sensor readings can fluctuate as clouds pass or the phone moves. The app applies exponential smoothing to reduce rapid variation in the displayed readings and includes short-term prediction to give a near-future view alongside the current estimate.

i

Smoothing and prediction make readings easier to follow; they do not remove sensor error or guarantee future weather conditions.

03 / CALIBRATION

Adjust the estimate to your device

Phones can differ in sensor response. Manual calibration options let you adjust the minimum and maximum settings and luminous efficacy used by the conversion, so you can tune the estimate for your device and intended use.

Keep a note of any adjustments you make. Changing calibration values changes the estimate; it does not independently verify the physical accuracy of the phone's sensor.

04 / READING TOOLS

Inspect the readings and orientation

Sensor diagnostics provide access to recent lux readings and sensor-orientation information. These tools help you check whether the phone is receiving light-sensor input and how its orientation is being interpreted during use.

05 / PRIVACY

Designed for offline use

The app's core features work fully offline, and its measurement workflow uses the device's sensors. No account is required. The project listing describes the app as not collecting user-identifying data and not using third-party tracking or data collection.

For the latest disclosures and the developer's current data-safety information, consult the Google Play listing.

06 / ACCURACY

Understanding the limitations

Readings depend on the phone's light and motion sensors, their placement and characteristics, the selected calibration values, device orientation, and the surrounding environment. Cases, shading, reflections, sensor differences, and changing sky conditions can affect results.

Use the app for estimation and comparison. For research, safety-critical work, or measurements requiring traceable accuracy, use an appropriately calibrated instrument.
